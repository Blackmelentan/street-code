Get Started
What Brings You Here?

Six account types share one sign-up flow: vehicle owner, collector, fleet company, garage or mechanic, parts supplier, and driver. Each unlocks a different set of tools, but all of them write to the same underlying vehicle and identity records.

This is a deliberate choice: rather than building six separate apps, Street Code is one platform with role-based views. A garage owner who also owns a personal car sees both worlds without switching accounts.

Get Started
Verify Your Business

No garage, mechanic, or supplier goes live without a human reviewing their business license and owner ID first. This is the first checkpoint in a marketplace where strangers pay other strangers to work on their car.

Review typically takes under 48 hours. The goal is friction low enough that legitimate businesses don't bounce off the process, but real enough that verification actually means something to a customer choosing who to trust.

Vehicle Backbone
My Garage

The owner's home screen: every vehicle they have, current mileage, and whether anything's due. Two vehicles here (a Land Cruiser flagged for service, a CR-V that's current) is exactly the kind of at-a-glance status a paper logbook never gave anyone.

Quick actions sit right at the bottom: log mileage, request a job, add a vehicle, or jump into the personal collection view. Nothing here requires more than one tap to start.

Vehicle Backbone
My Garage, First Use

A brand-new account with zero vehicles is the single most common first screen anyone will see. It has to explain the value of adding a vehicle without feeling like a wall.

The copy makes a specific promise: even a used car with no formal service history starts building one the day it's added, which matters most in a market where a lot of vehicle history simply isn't recorded anywhere else.

Vehicle Backbone
Vehicle Passport

This is the public, shareable page for one vehicle, ownership history and every logged service event, plus mileage. It's the artifact a buyer checks before purchase, or a garage checks before quoting a job.

Every entry on this page was generated as a byproduct of normal platform use (a garage closing a job card, an owner logging mileage), not manually typed in after the fact. That's what makes it hard to fake.

Vehicle Backbone
Report Stolen / Missing

A report filed here doesn't sit in a queue somewhere, it broadcasts nationwide the moment it's submitted, visible to any officer running a Plate/VIN Lookup or Roadside check anywhere in the country.

This is the direct payoff of the Civic split: a consumer-side report instantly becomes law-enforcement-visible data, without the owner ever needing to know or care how that handoff works.

Insurance & Protection
Vehicle Insurance

Insurance status lives on the same record as everything else about the vehicle (provider, policy number, coverage type, and expiry) instead of a paper document that only exists in a glovebox.

The near-term version of this screen is a display layer over a real insurer's policy data. Getting there requires an actual underwriting partnership, the Insurance Partner Pitch document lays out exactly that conversation.

Insurance & Protection
Compare Insurers

This is the broker function: multiple real insurers quoting the same vehicle side by side, instead of a single relationship with one provider deciding what an owner pays.

A broker partnership means Street Code never has to pick a side between insurers competing for the same customer, which is exactly the kind of neutral position that keeps a marketplace trustworthy on both ends.

Insurance & Protection
Marketplace Protection

This is what makes it safe to pay a stranger through the app: funds sit in escrow and only release once the customer confirms a job or booking is actually done right. If it isn't, there's a claims path instead of a dead end.

This screen exists because trust is the actual product being sold in a two-sided marketplace, not the app itself. Nobody hands a mechanic they've never met money up front without something backing that decision.

Insurance & Protection
File a Claim

A claim starts pre-filled with the booking or job it's tied to, no retyping vehicle details, dates, or who was involved. Photo upload and a short description are the only real inputs needed.

Average resolution time is a number worth being honest about early and then beating in practice, trust in a claims process is built by consistently fast, fair outcomes, not by the promise of them.

Vehicle Backbone
Add a Vehicle

Scan a VIN plate or windshield sticker and the make, model, year, trim, and engine decode automatically. Manual entry is the fallback, not the default, because the whole platform runs better when the VIN is right from day one.

This is the single most important screen in the product from a data standpoint: everything else (passport, insurance, fleet ops, mobility listings) depends on the vehicle record started here being accurate.

Diagnostics & Tools
Connect Scan Tool

Pairing a real OBD-II Bluetooth adapter turns self-reported mileage and vague symptom descriptions into live, verified data, odometer reading and engine status pulled directly from the vehicle, not typed in by a person.

This is optional hardware, not a requirement to use the platform. It exists for owners and garages who want a level of certainty that manual logging can't provide, especially on higher-value vehicles.

Diagnostics & Tools
Fault Codes & Mileage

A live scan surfaces actual diagnostic trouble codes, not a guess at what might be wrong, and flags severity so a P0301 misfire reads differently from a minor EVAP leak.

The natural next step from this screen is a single tap into Request a Job, with the fault codes already attached. A garage quoting against real codes instead of a phone description quotes faster and more accurately.

Fleet & Driver Ops · Civic
Fleet Dashboard

A company's entire fleet in one view, status, driver assignment, and monthly cost, replacing what's usually a spreadsheet three people maintain inconsistently.

Compliance alerts surface automatically: an expiring insurance policy or an overdue inspection shows up here before it becomes a problem on the road, not after.

Fleet & Driver Ops · Civic
Staff & Drivers

Every driver, their license status, and what vehicle they're currently assigned to, in one list instead of a binder in someone's office drawer.

License expiry is flagged with enough lead time to actually renew before it lapses, the difference between a routine reminder and a vehicle pulled off the road mid-mission.

Fleet & Driver Ops · Civic
Pulse, Live Tracking

Pulse is the platform's live telemetry feature: route, speed, and status for a vehicle in real time, synced over Bluetooth from an OBD device. For tenant fleets, it's expected during working hours, for individual owners, it's off unless they turn it on.

Geofence alerts are the practical payoff for a fleet manager: a vehicle leaving its approved operating area shows up here immediately, not in an end-of-month report.

Fleet & Driver Ops · Civic
Vehicle Capacity Board

The question this screen answers directly: who has which vehicle right now, for what stated purpose, and who signed off on it. That's a question most fleets currently answer by asking around.

Every vehicle sits in exactly one state (available, on a mission, or in the shop) so there's no ambiguity about what's actually free to dispatch at any given moment.

Fleet & Driver Ops · Civic
Dispatch & Authorize

Checking a vehicle out requires a named requester, a stated mission, and a named authorizer before it moves, a deliberately small amount of friction in exchange for a fleet that's never unaccounted for.

The stepper (Requested, Authorizing, Checked Out, Returned) makes the current state of any dispatch visible to anyone looking, not just the two people directly involved.

Garage Marketplace
Find a Garage

Customers choose a garage the way they'd choose almost anything else now, rating, distance, and specialty on a map, instead of relying on whoever they happen to know.

This is also where a garage's reputation starts compounding: a shop that consistently does good work climbs in relevance for every customer searching nearby, not just the ones who already know its name.

Garage Marketplace
Compare Quotes

One job posted, multiple real quotes back, the customer sees price, rating, job count, and next available slot side by side, instead of calling three garages separately and trying to remember what each one said.

This is the core marketplace loop the entire Phase 1 launch is built around: prove that this comparison actually happens and actually closes jobs before building anything further out.

Garage Marketplace
Open Gigs Near You

Independent mechanics without a fixed shop can still find paid work here, this is the gig layer, distinct from a garage's own job card, and it's what turns a one-person operation into a real participant in the marketplace.

Jobs are filterable by distance, pay, or recency, and every one shows exactly what's being asked for before a mechanic spends time quoting it.

Garage Marketplace
Job Card

The garage's working view of a job, vehicle, customer, mileage in, diagnostic photos, parts used, labor hours, and a stepper showing exactly where the job stands right now.

Closing this job card is what actually writes a new entry to the vehicle's permanent service history. The record-keeping isn't a separate task a garage has to remember to do, it's a byproduct of doing the job through the app at all.

Garage Marketplace
Messages

Customer and garage stay in one thread, tied directly to the job, not scattered across phone calls, walk-ins, and separate WhatsApp chats that nobody can reference later.

This thread also becomes evidence if something needs to go to Dispute Resolution, a documented conversation is worth more than two conflicting accounts of what was agreed to.

Garage Marketplace
Rate the Garage

A completed job becomes a rating in one tap, the mechanism that makes the whole marketplace self-policing over time instead of relying on the platform to manually vouch for every shop.

Ratings, response time, and completion rate together are what let a new customer trust a garage they've never used, based on garages they've never met vouching for it through their own experience.

Parts Sourcing
Search Parts

Finding the right part starts from what actually fits the customer's specific vehicle, filtered by fitment first, then by OEM, aftermarket, or recycled, and by price.

"Fits my vehicle" is on by default, not an afterthought filter, because the single biggest failure mode in parts shopping is ordering the wrong thing for a vehicle that looks similar but isn't quite the same.

Parts Sourcing
Parts Catalog

Every listed part is matched against the vehicle's actual VIN, with multiple suppliers competing on price for the same fitment-confirmed item, OEM, aftermarket, and recycled shown side by side rather than forcing a choice upfront.

This is a real second revenue line beyond garage-job commissions, and it strengthens the garage relationship too: a shop that can source parts through the same app it's already using has one less reason to leave it.

Parts Sourcing
Checkout

Paying for parts works the same way as everything else on the platform, mobile money first, card as the fallback, because that's how this market actually pays for things, not how a Western fintech assumes it does.

Delivery estimate and cost are shown before payment, not revealed as a surprise afterward.

Mobility
Browse Vehicles

Every vehicle available to hire nearby, filtered by price, type, and, critically, whether its history is actually verified. That last filter doesn't exist on Turo or Getaround, because they don't have a service-history backbone to draw it from.

This is where the Vehicle Backbone pillar becomes a genuine competitive advantage in Mobility, not just a nice-to-have: trust that took months to build in the repair marketplace shows up automatically here.

Utilities & Services
Nearby

Fuel, EV charging, tires, panel beaters, and lodges in one filterable list, because a trip is never just about the vehicle itself, and splitting these into five separate screens would be exactly the kind of clutter worth avoiding.

Each category shares the same directory format, icon, name, distance, open status, so switching between what you're looking for doesn't mean relearning a new layout.

Utilities & Services
Routes & Directions

Turn-by-turn directions that already know what's useful along the way, a fuel station two minutes off-route shows up here without a separate search.

This is a deliberately light feature: the goal isn't to compete with a dedicated maps app, it's to remove the need to leave Street Code for a five-minute detour.

Mobility
Book a Vehicle

Booking a hire car with the trust layer built in from the start, verified service history, an owner rating, and now a Civic-backed safety check on both the vehicle and the person renting it.

Price breakdown is shown before payment, and mobile money is the lead payment option here too, Orange Money and MTN aren't relevant in this market, which is why this screen specifically uses Afrimoney and QMoney.

Mobility
Booking Confirmed

A confirmation that actually tells the renter what happens next, pickup location, what to expect at the condition check-in, and the return deadline, instead of just a checkmark and a receipt.

This matters more than it looks like it should: most booking abandonment and post-booking anxiety happens in the gap between paying and knowing exactly what to do next.

Mobility
New Ride Request

A driver sees the fare and trip distance before accepting, with a short countdown to respond, no surprises on either side about what a ride actually pays before committing to it.

This is the driver-side half of ride-hailing that a lot of early sketches of this product were missing entirely, the rider's booking screen is only half the marketplace.

Mobility
En Route to Drop-off

Live trip tracking for the driver, with rider details and ETA in one place, the same underlying map and telemetry infrastructure as Pulse on the fleet side, reused here for a completely different use case.

This is also what a rider's trusted contact could see if that safety feature gets built out, the groundwork for it already exists in this screen.

Mobility
Trip Complete

Earnings broken down transparently the moment a trip ends, fare, platform fee, and payout method, instead of a driver having to wait or calculate it themselves.

Daily trip count and total sit right below, because for a driver deciding whether to stay online for one more ride, that number matters more than almost anything else on the screen.

Browse Listings

Dealers, private sellers, and disposal auctions in one list, filterable by source, because a buyer comparing options doesn't think in separate categories, they think in "what's out there right now."

Every listing carries the same verified passport data the rest of the platform already built. A private seller's used Land Cruiser shows up with exactly as much trust signal as a dealership's, which is the whole point of building the Vehicle Backbone first.

Vehicle Sales Marketplace
List My Vehicle

A private seller doesn't retype anything: mileage, VIN, and service history pull directly from the vehicle's existing passport, since that record already exists the moment the vehicle was added to the platform.

This is the payoff for every mileage log and job card completed along the way, a listing that's instantly more credible than a classified ad with no history behind it.

Vehicle Sales Marketplace
Dealer Inventory

A dealership's stock managed like inventory should be: listed, pending, and sold, with total inventory value visible at a glance instead of buried in a separate spreadsheet.

Dealers are a distinct account type from private sellers on purpose, higher volume, different verification requirements, and a business relationship worth managing differently from a one-time individual listing.

Personal Collection & Car Culture
Feed

One feed (builds, meets, recalls, and industry tips) blended together the way a real social feed works, instead of splitting car culture and news into tabs nobody will bother checking separately.

This is the retention engine for the whole platform. Booking a repair is a once-a-year event; scrolling a feed about cars you actually care about is a daily habit, and every post here still runs through the same vehicle-record backbone underneath.

Personal Collection & Car Culture
My Collection

A collector's vehicles valued like a portfolio, not just listed like inventory, with a breakdown showing what share of total value each car represents.

This is a fundamentally different use case from Fleet Ops even though both involve multiple vehicles: one is about operational cost and compliance, the other is about a collection people are genuinely emotionally invested in.

Personal Collection & Car Culture
Collection Profile

The page people actually want to show off, cover video, follower count, and a photo/video grid organized by car, built on the same verified vehicle data as everything else on the platform.

This is the screen that answers "why would I open this app for fun, not just when something breaks", and it's the one most likely to get shared outside the platform entirely.

Personal Collection & Car Culture
Cars & Coffee Banjul

A car meet with real details (date, host, attendee count, what to expect) instead of a flyer photo circulating on WhatsApp with half the information missing.

Garages and parts suppliers sponsoring events like this is a natural, non-intrusive way for the marketplace side of the platform to reach people through the community side, without it ever feeling like an ad.

Account & Infrastructure
Notifications

Everything that needs a reaction (a completed job, a compliance alert, a message, someone liking a build post) in one list instead of scattered across push notifications from six different app sections.

The mix here is intentional: operational alerts and social ones sit together, because that's genuinely how people check a phone, one list at a time.

Account & Infrastructure
Payments

A wallet that shows exactly what came in, what went out, and when the next payout lands, for a garage, a driver, or anyone else earning through the platform.

Withdrawal defaults to mobile money because that's the fastest, most-felt benefit for a cash-driven business, faster payout speed is worth leading with over almost any other feature in this pillar.

Platform Admin · Civic
Verification Queue

Nobody goes live on the platform (garage, supplier, or mechanic) without a human reviewing their documents first. This is where that review actually happens, with pending, verified, and flagged counts visible at a glance.

Missing documents don't get an automatic rejection, "Request Info" keeps a legitimate but incomplete application moving instead of dead-ending it.

Platform Admin · Civic
Dispute Resolution

When a job goes wrong, the claim, the counter-claim, and the actual evidence (chat logs, photos, invoices) live in one place instead of scattered across whoever happened to save what.

The decision here is binary and consequential: refund the customer or release funds to the garage. That weight is exactly why this screen exists separately from a general support inbox.

Platform Admin · Civic
Appeals & Overrides

The legitimate path for reversing a violation, suspension, or dispute outcome, every decision here requires a typed reason before it can be submitted, no exceptions.

This screen exists specifically as the alternative to an undisclosed backdoor: real control over outcomes, exercised by a named person, for a stated reason, on the record.

Platform Admin · Civic
Audit Log

Every admin action (appeal decisions, verification approvals, dispute resolutions) logged with who did it, when, and why, and structurally impossible to edit or delete afterward, including by platform operators.

This is the record that makes every override defensible if it's ever questioned, by a regulator, a court, or just an angry user. Without it, every admin decision is just someone's word.

Law Enforcement Access · Civic
Plate / VIN Lookup

A scan returns exactly what an officer is legally entitled to know (registration, insurance status, stolen flag, outstanding violations), nothing more, and nothing hidden from the record of the check itself.

This screen is sold separately to law enforcement as part of Street Code Civic, entirely apart from the consumer app, specifically so it never becomes a reason for regular vehicle owners to distrust the product they use every day.

Law Enforcement Access · Civic
Stolen Vehicle Alerts

Every active report shows up here nationwide, the moment an owner files one, visible to any officer running a check anywhere in the country, not just the precinct closest to where it happened.

Marking a vehicle recovered closes the loop and clears the flag everywhere at once, so a recovered vehicle doesn't keep getting stopped weeks after the fact.

Law Enforcement Access · Civic
Roadside Verification

A scanned passport returns a fast pass/fail checklist at a stop, registration, insurance, flags, cutting what used to require a radio call and paperwork down to seconds.

Any further search requires the officer to specify the type of search being conducted and notify the driver before it happens, a procedural safeguard built into the product, not left to be assumed.

Law Enforcement Access · Civic
Resolve Violation

A recorded violation doesn't automatically mean a fine, a short in-app course is offered as an alternative resolution, which is a genuinely better outcome for a first-time or minor issue than punitive-only enforcement.

The consequence for ignoring it entirely is stated plainly: unresolved after 30 days risks a license suspension visible nationwide to any officer performing a check, consequential, but never hidden or arbitrary.

Account & Infrastructure
Payment Methods

Every way someone can get paid or pay in, managed from one place, Afrimoney and QMoney as the primary mobile money rails, cards as the secondary option for tourism-driven bookings.

A default payment method is always set, so checkout never stalls on a decision that should have already been made ahead of time.

Account & Infrastructure
Settings

Privacy defaults matter here specifically: Pulse live-data sharing and public collection profiles are both off unless explicitly turned on by the individual owner, never assumed, never bundled into a broader terms-of-service checkbox.

Currency selection (GMD, USD, EUR, GBP) sits right alongside, a small detail, but the right one for a platform launching in a market where several currencies genuinely circulate day to day.
